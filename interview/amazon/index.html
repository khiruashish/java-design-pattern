    Interview

    Amazon: Design Patterns in Interviews¶

    Commonly reported, not official

    Based on Amazon's public interview guidance and widely shared candidate experiences. Details vary by team and level. Confirm with your recruiter.

    What the process commonly looks like¶

    • An online assessment (coding, sometimes work-style questions) or phone screen, then an interview loop (often 4–5 interviews).
    • Rounds cover coding, object-oriented / low-level design (common for SDE I/II), system design (SDE II and above), and behavioral questions in every round.
    • Amazon publishes its Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Dive Deep, Bias for Action, and others) and its interview guidance says behavioral questions are based on them. One interviewer, often called the Bar Raiser, is from outside the hiring team.

    Where design patterns show up¶

    Situation Pattern knowledge that helps
    LLD: "design a locker system / parking lot / vending machine" State, Strategy, Factory Method, Singleton (and its pitfalls)
    Extensibility follow-ups ("now add a new payment type") Open/closed with Strategy/Factory, Decorator
    System design (orders, inventory, notifications) Saga, Outbox, Idempotent Consumer, CQRS, queues
    Operational excellence Circuit Breaker, Bulkhead, Rate Limiter, Tracing

    What interviewers look for¶

    Signal What strong looks like
    LLD Clear entities and responsibilities, extensible where requirements will change, working code for the core flow
    System design Requirements and scale first, simple design that works, then bottlenecks, failure modes and cost
    Leadership Principles Specific STAR stories with your actions, data, and results; real trade-offs and mistakes
    Ownership Talks about operating what you build: monitoring, on-call, fixing root causes

    Prepare two stories per Leadership Principle

    Interviewers ask follow-ups ("what data did you use?", "what would you do differently?"). Each STAR tie-in at the end of the pattern pages in this handbook is a template; replace it with your own experience.

    15 practice questions (with model answers)¶

    1. Design an Amazon Locker-style package locker system.

    Lockers with sizes (S/M/L), allocation Strategy (smallest fitting), codes with expiry, locker State (free/reserved/occupied), notifications via Observer; concurrency on allocation.

    2. Design a vending machine.

    State machine: Idle → HasMoney → Dispensing; coin handling; out-of-stock; refunds. See the Vending Machine LLD.

    3. Your checkout calls payment, inventory and shipping. How do you keep them consistent?

    Orchestrated Saga with compensations, Outbox for events, idempotent handlers, timeouts and PENDING states.

    4. How do you avoid charging a customer twice when the client retries?

    Idempotency-Key stored with the response; unique constraints; replay the stored response. See Idempotent Consumer.

    5. Add a new discount type to a pricing engine without modifying existing code.

    Each discount is a Strategy/Specification; a registry or chain applies them; new type = new class + registration. See the Discount Engine LLD.

    6. Design a notification service for order updates (email, SMS, push).

    Channel Strategy + Factory, templates (Template Method), preferences, retries and DLQ, dedupe keys, rate limits. See the Notification LLD.

    7. Your service depends on a slow third-party API. How do you protect it?

    Timeouts, Circuit Breaker with fallback, Bulkhead, caching, async processing via queues.

    8. Design a system to show 'customers who bought this also bought'.

    Event stream of orders → batch/stream job computing co-occurrence → read model per product (CQRS) in a key-value store, cached at the edge.

    9. Singleton: when is it acceptable and what are the risks?

    For truly single, stateless or immutable resources (config, registries) via DI; risks are hidden global state, testing pain, and false assumptions of uniqueness across JVMs. See Singleton.

    10. Design an inventory service that never oversells during a flash sale.

    Atomic conditional decrements (UPDATE ... SET qty = qty - 1 WHERE qty > 0), reservations with TTL, a queue for the hottest SKUs, rate limits per user, and reconciliation.

    11. How would you find the cause of rising p99 latency in production?

    Metrics to find which endpoint, tracing to find the slow hop, logs for that trace; check recent deploys and dependencies (Dive Deep).

    12. Observer vs. pub/sub messaging?

    Observer is in-process and synchronous by default; pub/sub uses a broker for decoupled, asynchronous, durable delivery across services. See Event-Driven.

    13. Design a parking garage pricing system that changes per hour, weekend and vehicle type.

    Composable pricing rules (Strategy + Chain or Decorator), config-driven rates, injectable clock for tests.

    14. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your team's design (Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit).

    STAR: your data-driven argument, how you disagreed respectfully, the decision, how you committed, and the outcome either way.

    15. Tell me about a production issue you owned end to end (Ownership, Dive Deep).

    STAR: detection, mitigation first, root cause with evidence, long-term fix (tests, alarms, runbooks), and what you changed in the team's process.

    4-week plan¶

    Week Do
    1 Coding fundamentals; 2 LLD problems (Parking Lot, Vending Machine) with working code
    2 4 more LLD problems; patterns: State, Strategy, Observer, Factory, Decorator
    3 System design: orders, inventory, notifications; Saga, Outbox, Idempotency, Circuit Breaker
    4 2 STAR stories per Leadership Principle; mock loop with a "bar raiser" style interviewer
